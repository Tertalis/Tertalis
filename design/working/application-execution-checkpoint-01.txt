Purpose:
    Records the agreed direction for platform-owned continuous Application
    execution: what drives an Application's work after its lifecycle hooks
    return, how it waits without polling, how external input reaches it, and
    how a host pauses it. AI Buddy is the first consumer; its application-owned
    host loop (host/buddyLoop.py) is the pattern this generalizes. The file-level
    plan is design/working/application-execution-implementation-plan-01.txt.

Decided inputs (design owner, 2026-11-27):
    - Step contract (not a timer API).
    - Debugger breakpoints stay Application-owned (AI Buddy) for now; the
      experience feeds later platform breakpoints (DA-18).
    - Exactly one executor per executing Application (ApplicationRun).
    - Future, not now: with many Applications running, non-important ones may
      be asked to go quiescent while important ones keep running; "I can go
      quiescent" becomes an AppPack manifest and/or config declaration.
    - Process: this checkpoint and a plan, then a platform branch for
      Integration review.


1. SCOPE
========

1.01. In scope (first slice):
    a. continuous execution after create/load, started by the platform;
    b. waking at the time the Application asks for, without polling;
    c. external input delivered as queued work that wakes the Application;
    d. host pause/resume at Job boundaries, with inspection still possible;
    e. a platform clock Applications and tests read instead of wall time
       passed in payloads;
    f. waiting for, or being called back on, a Job's completion.

1.02. Not in this slice:
    - Application quiescence/suspension proper (G6; resource release, model
      unload, wake triggers). Recorded as future in section 9.
    - Long external waits without holding the ApplicationRun lane (G1e; the
      Flow Accepted/continuation shape, checkpoint 11 section 66).
    - A general scheduler, workflow engine or cross-Application arbitration
      (checkpoint 11 section 108: not until patterns emerge).
    - Persisted timers or continuation across ApplicationRuns: due times live
      in Application state; a new run simply steps at start.
    - Platform breakpoints (DA-18).


2. THE EXECUTOR
===============

2.01. Each ApplicationRun whose AppPack declares a step capability has exactly
one executor. It is part of the run's realization, created at run start and
stopped before Pack unload. An AppPack without a step declaration keeps today's
caller-driven behaviour (no executor), so existing Applications are unaffected.

2.02. The executor runs Jobs one at a time on the ApplicationRun lane, in this
order of preference at each boundary:
    1. submitted Jobs, first in first out;
    2. the step Job, when one is due.
Submitted work therefore always reaches the Application before its own next
step, matching AI Buddy's "input before work" rule.

2.03. Hosts and other threads interact only through thread-safe requests:
    submit(capability, payload) -> Job      queue work (external input, developer
                                            interventions); wakes the executor
    wake()                                  run a step soon (reason "wake")
    pause() / resume()                      stop / restart starting new Jobs at
                                            the next boundary; the running Job
                                            finishes
    snapshot()                              phase, current Job, queued count,
                                            next step time, last fault
Reads that are not Jobs (invokeCapability of read-only capabilities) keep
working while the executor is idle, sleeping or paused, serialized by the lane.

2.04. Phases: starting, running (a Job is executing), sleeping (until a time),
idle (until an event), paused (by the host), faulted (a step failed or returned
an invalid outcome; behaves like paused until resumed), stopping, stopped.

2.05. Cancellation is per Job (DA-19): the host keeps the Job handle from
submit() or from snapshot() and requests cancellation; the executor does not
invent its own interrupt. Stopping the executor at run shutdown requests
cancellation of nothing by default: it stops starting work and waits a bounded
time for the running Job; the host may cancel it explicitly.


3. THE STEP CONTRACT
====================

3.01. The AppPack declares one step capability. The executor invokes it as an
ordinary Job (OrchestrationUnit, transaction, cancellation) with payload:
    {"reason": "start" | "event" | "due" | "continue" | "wake" | "resume",
     "now": <platform clock, ISO-8601 with offset>}

3.02. The step does at most one bounded unit of the Application's own work and
returns exactly one outcome:
    {"next": "now"}               more work is ready: step again at the next
                                  boundary (after any submitted work)
    {"next": "at", "at": <ISO>}   sleep until that time unless an event or
                                  wake arrives first
    {"next": "idle"}              sleep until an event or wake
An invalid outcome or a failed step Job faults the executor (2.04); the
Application's committed state is unaffected beyond what the Job committed.

3.03. Events: completion of any submitted Job counts as an event and makes a
step due (reason "event"). Submitted Jobs are the only event source in this
slice; Flow Invocations and Subscriptions route into the same queue later.

3.04. Time: "at" is wall-clock (the Application's domain time). The executor
converts it to a monotonic wait using the clock, re-checks at most every 60 s
so a clock change cannot strand it, and never polls the Application to find
out whether work is due.

3.05. What a step is for the Application is Application meaning. For AI Buddy
one step is one phase of its cycle (prepare, execute, process, deliver), chosen
from its committed state; a developer breakpoint is simply a step outcome of
"idle" until a continue arrives as a submitted Job (2.03, 3.03).


4. DELIVERY OF RESULTS TO THE HOST
==================================

4.01. Applications already publish what the outside world should see through
their own state and capabilities (AI Buddy's outbox). The executor adds no
output channel. A host observes Job completion (5) and reads capabilities.

4.02. The browser frontend's delivery seam (browser-frontend-checkpoint-01 3.03)
becomes: frontend Invocations are submitted as Jobs (or mapped to Application
requests that submit them); the loop owner role moves from Application host
code to the executor.


5. JOB COMPLETION
=================

5.01. Job gains wait(timeout) and addDoneCallback(callback). Callbacks run
after the terminal state is recorded, outside runtime lanes, on the executing
thread; exceptions in callbacks are isolated and traced, never change the Job.

5.02. submit() returns the Pending Job immediately; its lifecycle is the
ordinary DA-19 lifecycle (queued means Pending).


6. CLOCK
========

6.01. The RuntimeHost owns a Clock (now() with local offset; monotonic() for
waits). Tests inject a controllable clock. CodeEntries read ctx.clock.now().
The executor stamps "now" into step payloads from the same clock.

6.02. Applications that pass time in payloads today (AI Buddy) may keep doing
so; ctx.clock is the source going forward.


7. LIFECYCLE INTEGRATION
========================

7.01. Create/load and hooks are unchanged. After onApplicationRun returns, the
executor starts and steps with reason "start".

7.02. Run shutdown stops the executor first (bounded wait for the running Job),
then the existing Pack cleanup and save ordering continue. A step that is
running when the host stops is not repeated by the next run; the Application's
own state decides what continues (05.05 of architecture: an uncertain completion
is not permission to repeat).


8. TRACING
==========

8.01. Executor phase changes, step outcomes, faults, submissions and wake
reasons are traced with applicationRunId and jobId. Step payloads and results
are not traced beyond their outcome kind and time.


9. FUTURE (RECORDED, NOT IN THIS SLICE)
=======================================

9.01. Quiescence eligibility: an AppPack (manifest) and/or the Application
config declares whether it may be asked to go quiescent and how important it
is to keep running. When many Applications run, the host may request
non-important ones to go quiescent while important ones continue. The executor
phases are the natural place for the quiescent state; G6's design governs what
quiescence releases.

9.02. Platform breakpoints (DA-18): AI Buddy's pause ids, stale-pause handling
and prompt/output edit points are the first experience to generalize.

9.03. Long waits off the lane (G1e): a step that starts external work could
return Accepted-style continuation instead of holding the lane until the model
finishes.

9.04. Flow event sources: Invocations and Subscription Emits feed the same
queue as submitted Jobs.


10. OPEN QUESTIONS
==================

10.01. Declaration form: manifest field on the AppPack (proposed
"application": {"step": "<capabilityId>"}) versus a lifecycle hook name. The
manifest form keeps room for 9.01.
10.02. Whether submit() should accept only capabilities of the run's own
AppPack in this slice (proposed yes).
10.03. Bounded wait at shutdown (proposed 30 s, configurable by the host).
10.04. Whether a faulted executor should retry automatically (proposed no).
