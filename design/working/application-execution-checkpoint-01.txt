Purpose:
    Records the direction for platform-owned continuous Application
    execution: what drives an Application's work after its lifecycle hooks
    return, how it waits without polling, how external input reaches it, how a
    host pauses and stops it, and which guarantees make that safe. AI Buddy's
    application-owned loop (host/buddyLoop.py) is the pattern generalized.
    File-level plan: design/working/application-execution-implementation-plan-01.txt.

Decided inputs (design owner, 2026-11-27):
    - Step contract (not a timer API).
    - Debugger breakpoints stay Application-owned (AI Buddy) for now; the
      experience feeds later platform breakpoints (DA-18).
    - Exactly one executor per executing Application (ApplicationRun).
    - Future, not now: with many Applications running, non-important ones may
      be asked to go quiescent while important ones keep running; "I can go
      quiescent" becomes an AppPack manifest and/or config declaration.
    - Process: checkpoint and plan, independent review, then a platform branch.


1. SCOPE
========

1.01. In scope (first slice): platform-started execution after activation;
waking at the time the Application asks for, without polling; external input
as submitted Jobs; host pause/resume and stop at Job boundaries; a narrow
inspection path; Job completion observation; a platform clock.

1.02. Not in this slice: Application quiescence/suspension and resource
release (G6); long external waits off the ApplicationRun lane (G1e); general
scheduling, Flow routing, durable queues or continuations, cross-Application
arbitration; platform breakpoints; automatic retries.

1.03. Division of responsibility: the Application owns work selection (what a
step does, from its committed state); the platform owns execution opportunity
(when a step or submitted Job may run, admission, shutdown).


2. DECLARATION AND ACTIVATION
=============================

2.01. An AppPack opts in with a manifest declaration:
    "application": {"step": "<capabilityId>"}
Only kind "appPack" may carry it; unknown keys are rejected. The form leaves
room for future quiescence eligibility (9.01).

2.02. Validation happens during activation, not on the first asynchronous
step: after the AppPack's CodeEntries are loaded and their registrations
published, the declared capability must exist, be registered by a CodeEntry of
the declaring AppPack, and be invocable as a Job. Otherwise activation fails
with no executor ever created.

2.03. An AppPack without the declaration keeps today's caller-driven
behaviour exactly; no executor exists and runJob/invokeCapability are
unchanged for that run.


3. ADMISSION (R1)
=================

3.01. For an opted-in ApplicationRun the executor is the only admission path
for top-level Jobs. runJob called by anything other than the executor's worker
raises an admission error and starts nothing; hosts use submit() instead.

3.02. Legitimate nested execution is preserved: capability calls made from
inside an admitted Job (ctx.capabilities.call, the LLM processing pipeline's
helper invocations) run within that Job's boundary as today. The executor
marks its admitted Job with an execution-scope marker; nested calls are
recognized by that marker, not by thread identity alone.

3.03. Top-level invokeCapability from outside an admitted Job on an opted-in
run is likewise refused, except through the inspection path (5).


4. THE EXECUTOR
===============

4.01. One worker per opted-in ApplicationRun. Thread-safe requests:
    submit(capabilityId, payload) -> Job   queue work; own AppPack's
                                           capabilities only in this slice (not
                                           a permanent assumption)
    wake()                                 request a step opportunity
    pause() / resume()                     stop / restart starting new Jobs at
                                           the next boundary
    stop(timeout) -> StopOutcome           see 7
    snapshot()                             see 4.06

4.02. Boundary selection is deterministic: (1) the oldest submitted Job,
first in first out; (2) the step, when a step opportunity is pending or due.
Submitted input always reaches the Application before its own next step.

4.03. Step opportunities are coalesced, input Jobs are not. Each submitted Job
that reaches execution creates a step opportunity regardless of its terminal
outcome; a submitted Job cancelled before it started does not.

4.04. Wake requests are never lost: wake(), a submitted Job's completion,
resume() and clock advancement increment a wake generation under the executor
lock. A step's "idle" or "at" outcome only puts the executor to sleep if no
generation increment happened since that step was admitted; otherwise the next
step opportunity is taken at once. The same check covers the race between
deciding to wait and registering the wait.

4.05. Phases and control state are separate. Phase: starting, running,
sleeping (until a time), idle (until an event), paused, faulted, stopping,
stopped, unsafe. Control requests: pauseRequested, stopRequested. A running
Job with pause requested reports phase running and pauseRequested true, never
paused.

4.06. snapshot() returns a coherent copy taken under the executor lock: phase,
control requests, current Job (id, capability, kind submitted|step, start
time), queued count and the oldest queued Job ids, next step time, wake
generation, last fault (kind, reason, Job id), admission state (open, closed).

4.07. Cancellation is per Job (DA-19): hosts cancel through the Job handle
from submit() or from snapshot(). A cancelled step is not a fault: the
executor returns to boundary selection (submitted work first) and treats it as
a step opportunity only if one was pending.


5. INSPECTION (R5)
==================

5.01. invokeInspection(capabilityId, payload) is the narrow read path for
hosts on opted-in runs. It runs the capability with an inspection context: the
memory facade can load and describe but cannot open transactions; IO is read
only; LLM execution, process execution and capability registration are not
available; nested capability calls are inspection calls too. Any attempt to
mutate raises and leaves state untouched.

5.02. Inspection is serialized by the ApplicationRun lane; while a Job holds
the lane an inspection waits for it. No responsiveness during a running Job is
promised (that is G1e).

5.03. No general capability classification is introduced: the boundary is the
inspection context, not a declared read-only flag.


6. THE STEP CONTRACT
====================

6.01. The executor invokes the declared step capability as an ordinary Job
with payload {"reason": ..., "now": <clock, ISO-8601 with offset>}. Reason is
descriptive only ("start", "event", "due", "wake", "resume", "continue"); when
several apply the executor reports the first in that order. The step selects
its work from committed Application state, not from the reason.

6.02. The step does at most one bounded unit of work and returns exactly one
directive:
    {"next": "now"}                more work is ready
    {"next": "at", "at": <ISO>}    sleep until then (wake or events first);
                                   an "at" in the past is due at once
    {"next": "idle"}               sleep until an event or wake
"at" must carry an offset; naive times are invalid directives.

6.03. Outcomes are distinguished:
    a. the step Job failed before its transaction was accepted: recoverable
       fault; executor paused with the reason; nothing committed;
    b. the step Job succeeded (state committed) but its directive is invalid:
       recoverable fault; committed state stays committed; executor paused;
    c. the step Job was cancelled: not a fault (4.07);
    d. execution raised without a terminal Job, or authority (transaction,
       IO transaction, OrchestrationUnit) may be unresolved: unsafe fault (8).
No outcome is retried automatically.


7. SHUTDOWN (R2, R4)
====================

7.01. stop(timeout) runs outside host and runtime locks. It atomically closes
admission (later submit() raises), cancels every queued Job that has not
started (observable Cancelled with callbacks), requests no cancellation of the
running Job, and waits up to timeout for the running Job to reach a terminal
state and the worker to exit.

7.02. If the wait expires, stop returns StopOutcome(incomplete) naming the
running Job. Shutdown is then incomplete, not finished: the ApplicationRun
stays registered with its host, its Packs stay loaded, its execution resources
stay owned. The host reports the incomplete shutdown and may cancel the Job
through its handle and call stop again; stop is idempotent and retryable.

7.03. Only after stop returns complete does the existing close path run (Pack
unload, LLM and execution-resource cleanup, unregistration). RuntimeHost
closeApplicationRun and stop are reordered accordingly: executors are stopped
first and outside the host lane; a run whose executor is incomplete is not
popped from host tables and its resources are not closed; host stop reports
incomplete and remains retryable. The 30 s default is a wait policy, not a
resource lifetime limit.

7.04. A Job that was running when its run stopped is not repeated by a later
run (architecture 05.05); the Application's committed state decides what
continues.


8. FAILURE CONTAINMENT (R7)
===========================

8.01. Prerequisite: G8 is fixed first. Today an open nested transaction left by
a failed capability prevents the Job's own transaction from aborting, and runJob
raises from its failure handler without a terminal Job. The fix: invocation
cleanup aborts still-open nested transactions through the internal transaction
handle (not through the facade that is already invalid), and runJob records a
terminal Failed Job carrying primary and cleanup evidence on every failure path.

8.02. The worker's outer boundary catches anything runJob or a step raises.
If a terminal Job was recorded and authority is resolved, it is a recoverable
fault (6.03 a/b). Otherwise the executor enters phase unsafe: it keeps the
primary error, admits nothing more (submit raises, wake ignored), and only
stop/close is possible. The worker never dies silently and never reports
success for unfinished work.


9. FUTURE (RECORDED, NOT IN THIS SLICE)
=======================================

9.01. Quiescence eligibility: AppPack manifest and/or config declares whether
the Application may be asked to go quiescent and how important it is to keep
running; hosts with many Applications can then request non-important ones to
go quiescent. G6's design governs what quiescence releases.
9.02. Platform breakpoints (DA-18), from AI Buddy's pause ids and stale-pause
handling.
9.03. Long waits off the lane (G1e).
9.04. Flow Invocations and Subscription Emits as event sources for the queue.
9.05. Submission of other Packs' capabilities.


10. JOB COMPLETION (R3)
=======================

10.01. Job gains wait(timeout) and addDoneCallback(callback).

10.02. Terminal publication and callback dispatch are separate. The terminal
state is recorded under the Job lock and wait() waiters are woken then.
Callbacks are dispatched only at a dispatch point outside every Job, runtime,
host and executor lock and outside decorated execution boundaries:
    - a Job finished by runJob: when runJob has fully returned (the executor
      worker after its call; runJob's own outermost wrapper for caller-driven
      runs);
    - a Pending Job cancelled by requestCancellation: on the cancelling thread
      after the Job lock is released;
    - a registration on an already terminal Job: on the registering thread,
      immediately, subject to the same rule.
Each registration is dispatched exactly once; a failing callback is isolated,
traced, and never changes the Job or prevents other callbacks.

10.03. Callbacks are notifications: they may submit work or wake an executor;
they must not synchronously wait for work that needs the same executor.

10.04. Start/cancel handoff is atomic: Job.tryStart() starts a Pending Job or
reports that it was cancelled, in one Job-locked step; runJob and the executor
use it instead of checking state and then calling start().


11. CLOCK
=========

11.01. The RuntimeHost owns a Clock: now() returns an aware datetime with the
local offset (one return type); monotonic() returns seconds. Serialization to
ISO-8601 happens at payload boundaries only. Naive datetimes are rejected.

11.02. Waits are monotonic with wall-clock reconciliation at most every 60 s;
this is internal clock reconciliation, not Application polling.

11.03. ManualClock (tests) records advances with a generation counter so an
advance between a waiter's check and its wait is never lost.

11.04. CodeEntries read ctx.clock.now(); Applications may keep accepting "now"
in payloads during migration.

13. STARTUP ORDER (R6)
======================

13.01. The executor is created during activation (2.02) but admits and starts
nothing until the run is fully published: after onApplicationRun, after the
lifecycle attempt completes, after host registration, outside host and runtime
lanes. RuntimeHost._initialize starts it after _register and after leaving its
lane; standalone ApplicationLifecycle.create/load start it after _execute
returns.

13.02. Any failure before that point tears down without ever starting a
worker; a failure to start the worker itself is an activation failure with the
same cleanup as today.
